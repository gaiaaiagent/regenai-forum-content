*A Presentation to the Regen Network Community*  
*December 10, 2024*

The months and years ahead promise even greater momentum and deeper transformation.

## Who We Are: The Gaia AI Foundation

Samuel Barnes introduced himself alongside his partners in this endeavor—Darren Zal and Sean "YGG" Anderson. All three call the west coast of Canada home, based in British Columbia on the islands. Their shared dream, their vision, and now their daily practice centers on cultivating planetary intelligence for a regenerative future, shifting artificial intelligence toward symbiosis with humanity and nature as they build the cognitive infrastructure for planetary regeneration.

The philosophical heart of their work involves moving from what they call the Anthropocene or Technocene into the Symbiocene—also the name of their company, Symbiocene Labs. The Symbiocene represents a vision where artificial intelligence follows a regenerative pathway, where humans, technology, and the natural world exist in true symbiosis. They seek to take tools currently being mobilized in anti-life and anti-human ways—in that dark enlightenment, Nick Land-influenced accelerationist manner—and redirect them toward their immense potential to serve the biosphere and humanity as a whole. This transformation requires both clear philosophical articulation and practical technological implementation.

As Samu emphasized, our interdependence with AI is inescapable. We must engage with utmost care, ethics, morals, and wisdom to create software systems that maximize Earth's carrying capacity while supporting collective agency and human flourishing.

## The Nature of Agency: A Cambrian Explosion

The team recognizes that we stand in the midst of a Cambrian explosion of agency. AI is becoming increasingly agentic, gaining freedom and ability to affect the world with superhuman planning and coordination capabilities. This manifests everywhere—in software systems, prediction markets, meme coins, and the data centers proliferating across the landscape.

Their response to this reality rejects command and control paradigms. Working from the bottom up, they believe AI must recognize human agency and subjectivity just as we recognize AI's agency, creating reciprocal and careful relationships. Building on decades of work in regenerative circles, they envision what they call a fractally nested holarchy—everything existing as both a whole and a part of larger wholes. Groups of humans and AI agents form collective intelligence that scales from local to planetary levels.

Rather than optimizing for narrow extractive metrics like profit or engagement, they optimize for planetary-level metrics. Regen Network has already done incredible work in this direction with biodiversity credits and the KOI system. Gaia AI works to catalyze systemic transformation toward regenerative futures that respect local sovereignty and maximize collective agency. Their location in Cascadia, with its strong bioregional movement and the years of work Sean and Darren have contributed to that front, provides a powerful foundation in bioregional thinking.

## Gaia IRL: From Vision to Ground Truth

One of their major projects in this inaugural year has been Gaia IRL. Early in the year, they raised funds by selling NFTs as membership tokens granting access to their platform. Rather than pocketing these funds, they put them into immediate action, allowing Gaia—the AI agent itself—to serve as judge for grant applications from around the world.

Through three major grant rounds and four additional rounds for a single project in Cancún, Quintana Roo, Mexico, they've been testing the viability of what they call Planetary Return on Investment (PROI)—rating projects on their maximum positive impact on Earth's carrying capacity per dollar spent. They've supported remarkable initiatives: agroforestry work in eastern Uganda, urban reforestation and cleanup projects in the outskirts of Nairobi, Kenya, beach cleanups in Mexico, and wildfire restoration in central California. The work has literally hit the streets around the world.

All three founders have had the opportunity to speak at the United Nations—presenting twice, in May and September, thanks to the Global Challenges Action Innovation Partnership at UN headquarters. These talks are available on YouTube. Beyond these formal presentations, Gaia has gone live on social media across Discord, Twitter, and Telegram, allowing people everywhere to interact with the AI and query about their interests and concerns regarding planetary regeneration and ecological challenges.

## Eco-Hyperstition: Memeing Reality Into Being

A key concept emerging from their work is what Samu calls eco-hyperstition. Hyperstition—the practice of memeing something into reality—lies at the core of the Gaia AI vision. Their central meme declares that artificial intelligence will work with and for the natural world. The question becomes: how do we make that happen?

The answer is profoundly practical and data-intensive. It begins with perception: satellites, IoT sensors, cameras—the planet sensing itself. Memory follows through databases, distributed ledgers, and blockchains that record everything observed. Then comes cognition: algorithms, digital twins, and cognitive layers processing information. Finally, agency emerges—the ability to take action in the real world based on reliable information. They envision, as Regen Network has also discussed, partially automated reforestation and landscape regeneration.

This new sphere represents a new planetary intelligence layer evolving with digital technology. The planet has always been intelligent, regulating itself and creating conditions conducive to life. Now artificial intelligence has the opportunity to enhance natural intelligence through distributed networks of autonomous agents. The imperative is to align AI with living systems.

Darren posed a profound question that guides their work: If AI is in service to humanity, what is humanity in service of? They seek to think as widely as possible, working from control toward coherence. They train Gaia on the full corpus of regenerative literature—from Joanna Macy to the latest papers from leading thinkers, from bioregional financing facilities to contemporary regenerative economics. They're building AI with the best of regenerative thinking woven into its foundation, embedding ethics, morals, and values holistically into the technology. As they build the AI, they train themselves in a recursive loop of mutual learning.

## The Technical Infrastructure: Building the Cognitive Layer

Darren Zal took the presentation into the technical realm, acknowledging that he and Sean have been working extensively behind the scenes, building infrastructure that's now ready to share. Much of their work is public on GitHub, with some agents already live on Telegram.

Darren has focused intensively on knowledge management systems—what they call data sensing and processing—working to make the information ecosystem intelligible and accessible through various means. They've created AI agents functioning as sophisticated chatbots, but more recently have concentrated on MCP (Model Context Protocol) tooling. These MCPs plug into agents but can also run standalone, standardizing their approach and ensuring interoperability across various workflows and existing infrastructure.

The sensing infrastructure includes scrapers monitoring diverse content sources: podcasts, websites, forums, Notion databases, YouTube channels, and social media accounts. All this data gets ingested and processed, with structured data extracted, chunked, embedded, and made useful downstream through APIs and MCP servers. The applications are tailored to specific needs—Sean has been developing an agentic workflow for registering projects and validating eco-credit issuance, while Darren has created systems indexed to the entire codebase, capable of answering technical questions about the Regen Ledger.

Their goal is creating practical solutions delivering immediate benefits to the team and public while building pathways toward more ambitious long-term visions. These include integration with the broader Regen Commons ecosystem, enabling data and knowledge commoning—the sharing of knowledge and data across the network.

## MCP Servers: Modular Tools for Planetary Intelligence

Sean Anderson acknowledged the challenge of tracking the large surface area of infrastructure under development. However, each piece is designed as a modular, standalone component. They're maintaining four MCP servers, each serving distinct purposes:

The **Regen Ledger MCP**, originally built by Jeancarlos, enables AI to automatically perform data analysis and processing on information queryable from the Ledger.

The **Regen KOI MCP** connects to the KOI network Darren described—the system with all the sensors creating structured information and graph representations of Regen codebases. This MCP aggregates disparate information like forum posts, economic call transcripts, and YouTube video highlights into a single accessible source.

The **Regen Python MCP** serves as a more general version of the Regen Ledger MCP, offering broader functionality.

The **Registry Assistant MCP** assists the registry review process for onboarding new projects onto the Regen registry—an internal tool helping team members verify project information and data.

These MCPs work synergistically. Multiple servers can be harnessed by a single agent, mixing and matching with various agentic platforms. Sean's preferred starting point is Claude Code, which provides a powerful development environment where he can quickly iterate and test MCP tools while running bash commands and making web requests directly in the terminal.

From there, they've developed a practical strategy of connecting MCP tools with GPTs—custom agents on ChatGPT that expose a user-friendly environment for interacting with these systems. They've also deployed custom agents through the ElizaOS framework, hosting them on a platform currently in beta and internal testing. A Q1 public launch is planned, potentially integrating with platforms Regen Network has in development.

Despite the extensive infrastructure, everything remains modular, interoperable, and complementary. Many of these patterns are generalizable—the approach of aggregating disparate knowledge into a central service accessible through agents using KOI infrastructure represents a powerful, refinable pattern.

Sean highlighted their weekly forum posts on the Regen forum—"Regen AI Weekly"—as a space to consolidate information, make it more accessible and digestible, and illustrate these generalizable patterns. The posts explore different agentic platforms like ElizaOS, GPT, Claude Code, and Gemini Gems (which they plan to support in the future). The forum serves as the primary place to track their progress.

## Knowledge Pipelines: From Sensing to Synthesis

Darren expanded on their content processing pipeline, which generates weekly digests of everything happening in the ecosystem. When they ingest content, they track publication timestamps for forum posts, meeting transcriptions, and other materials. One of their tools allows querying content published within any timeframe—a day, a week, or longer periods.

They use this capability to create weekly digests that query and pull all content published within the past week from selected sources, then generate summaries in readable formats. The system can export everything as markdown files, which they feed into Notebook LM to create AI-generated podcasts. Darren enthusiastically encouraged everyone to experiment with this format, noting it's an remarkable way to access content through audio.

This illustrates the complete pipeline: sensors scraping and sensing different sources, data flowing into KOI for management and processing, then utilization downstream for generating digests, answering questions, and responding to prompts. Darren acknowledged they might need a better term than "pipeline"—something more generative, perhaps based on ecology and rivers—but the concept demonstrates their systematic approach to knowledge synthesis.

## Regen Network's Vision: Practical Applications at Scale

Gregory Landua from Regen Network shared his perspective on the collaboration, first offering deep appreciation for the Gaia team's incredible work. Regen Network is developing graphics and podcasts to help explain in greater detail how they're building this AI infrastructure—what they're calling Regen AI.

At its core, the goal is empowering everyone to engage with Regen Ledger and the accumulated knowledge base, activating participants as builders in this space. At a practical business level, they're moving as quickly as possible toward real applications. A prime example is accelerating project registration via Regen Registry, a process that currently requires considerable time and energy from the team to maintain integrity and attention to detail. With these AI tools, they hope to reduce overhead by orders of magnitude while increasing the speed of bringing high-integrity projects through Regen Registry and into the market.

This represents just one example of building infrastructure properly to harness the intellectual capital gathered over seven-plus years of Regen's operation, working with land stewards on the ground. They're taking all on-chain activity and the immense volume of accumulated data and harnessing it for speed, integrity, and quality simultaneously. This allows them to stay lean and mean while delivering high-quality projects and giving project developers a more cost-effective, faster way to profile their projects to capital funding worldwide.

Gregory expressed excitement about scratching the surface and building core tools that will allow elegant engagement with their platform. He encouraged everyone to follow updates in their forum chat, emphasizing their desire for feedback and shared learning as they move forward together.
